Making engineering work on the factory floor.

I work across product development, component industrialization, tooling, suppliers and production transitions. I care about the decisions that take a design from a drawing to something we can actually manufacture, validate and build consistently.

Some of the work: stamped-part localization, design-for-manufacturing reviews, tooling changes and dishwasher subsystem development.

02 · master's research / environmental engineering

The gap between installed treatment capacity and actual throughput.

My thesis examines why installed waste-treatment capacity doesn't always translate into actual throughput. I model how equipment recovery, suitable feedstock and downstream limits determine how much waste gets processed.

What I built: audited-case analysis, exploratory capacity-recovery scenarios and a research dashboard that makes the assumptions visible.

A recurring theme
"I like the space between how things work and how they could work better."

I've moved between manufacturing, optics, thermal systems, plasma physics, product design and environmental engineering. On paper, that looks like a lot of different directions.

To me, the connecting thread is pretty simple: I enjoy understanding complicated systems, especially when there are people on the other side of the engineering decisions.

That doesn't mean every idea becomes a product. Some become research. Some become prototypes. Some stay as sketches in a notebook. All of them teach me something.

what i care about

Making room
for more people.

Good design should make life a little less difficult to navigate.

Accessibility is one of the themes I keep returning to. I've thought about haptic navigation glasses for blind and low-vision users, and ways to make movement inside homes without elevators less difficult for older adults.

These are early ideas, not tested assistive devices. And that distinction matters: useful accessibility design needs direct input from the people it's meant for, careful testing and attention to safety.

I want to keep learning how to build with people, rather than just designing for them from a distance.
