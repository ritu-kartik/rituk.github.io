midlife wellbeing

Care that doesn't need a cycle calendar.

If periods change unexpectedly, bleeding is unusually heavy, or bleeding occurs after menopause, seek medical assessment. Exercise choices should account for existing conditions and symptoms.
the everyday foundations

Good habits, with familiar foods.

Eat enough

Try dal, chana, rajma, eggs, fish or other protein with roti or rice and seasonal sabzi. Iron-rich foods matter when you bleed.

Move in ways that fit

Walking, dancing, household activity and simple strength exercises all count. Adjust for pain, energy and ability.

Protect recovery

Drink safe water, rest when needed and aim for consistent sleep. Expensive supplements are not required.

what this prototype does and doesn't do

Guidance, not hormone predictions.

The uploaded 28 Day Cycle Guide inspired the four-phase framing and food-first approach. Its strict phase-based diets, exercise restrictions and claims about diagnosing hormone problems from symptoms are not treated as established medical evidence here. This prototype does not diagnose, infer fertility, measure hormones or prescribe calorie targets.

For general guidance, see NHS menopause information, ACOG women's health and WHO physical activity guidance.
