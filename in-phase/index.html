Which part of your cycle?

Enter the first day of your last period to see a rough phase estimate. You can check the date in an existing period app. The guide below also works without any dates.

Start with the general food guide below, or add your dates for a phase estimate.

How are phases estimated?

The first day of bleeding is cycle day 1. Ovulation is sometimes estimated around 14 days before the next period, but timing varies considerably. A calendar estimate cannot identify fertile days reliably. Irregular cycles, pregnancy, breastfeeding, hormonal contraception and perimenopause can change the pattern.

          Three things worth knowing

          Eat regularly

          Pulses, grains, seasonal vegetables and fruit provide energy, protein, fibre and micronutrients. Eggs, fish or meat can be included if you eat them.

          Build strength and rest

          Walking and simple strength exercises support heart, muscle and bone health. Good sleep supports recovery and wellbeing.

          When to seek medical advice

          Get medical advice for severe or worsening period pain, unusually heavy bleeding, bleeding after menopause, or persistent fatigue and dizziness. Heavy bleeding can cause iron deficiency.

          The cycle estimate is approximate. Use effective contraception when avoiding pregnancy.
